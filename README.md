# BallotBridge
A plain-language toolbox for understanding ballot measures: what they say, what a yes or no vote changes, and who’s behind them. Tap any term for an explanation in your own language. Never tells you how to vote.

## Inspiration
My parents are immigrants and proud U.S. citizens. They take voting seriously, and every election they still ask me to help them understand what's on their ballot. Even with the Blue Book in hand, they got stuck on terms like "commissioner" and "mill levy," and the book itself is long and hard to get through.

I struggle too, and English is my first language. This year I read my ballot almost word for word and still missed things: a measure whose title sounded simple but whose wording reached further than I realized, and how little I knew about who had put most of the measures on the ballot. I only learned about it after the results came out.

Information about ballot measures is public, but it's buried. I wanted to build the tool my family actually needed: not one that tells anyone how to vote, but one that makes it easier to be informed.

## What it does
BallotBridge is a toolbox for understanding what's on your ballot, in layers:

- **Words:** Tap any confusing term to get a plain-English definition, an explanation of what it means *in this measure*, and that explanation in your own language.
- **The measure:** A plain-language summary next to the original text, with every sentence linked to the clause it comes from.
- **Yes vs. No:** A side-by-side comparison of what changes if the measure passes or fails, including where the money comes from, where it goes, how taxes change, and how hard it would be to undo.
- **The full picture:** Every effect of the measure, not just what the title suggests, each tied to the exact text that causes it.
- **Who's behind it:** Who put the measure on the ballot and who is funding each side, with links to public records.
- **Perspectives & questions:** Links to arguments from different sides, and a chat that answers follow-up questions using only cited sources.

BallotBridge never tells anyone how to vote. It describes what a measure does and who's behind it, and leaves the decision to the voter.
